# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

This repository is a learning and portfolio project for the Datheon Python
Programming Internship, and it is also a public place where people arrive at
different levels of experience. Everyone who takes part — as a contributor, a
reviewer, a reporter of a bug, or someone asking a question — can expect to be
treated with respect, and can expect their participation not to depend on who
they are.

We are committed to making participation harassment-free for everyone,
regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience,
education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race,
religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Behaviour that builds this project:

- Giving and accepting specific, technical feedback — about the code, not the
  person who wrote it.
- Assuming the other person is trying, and asking a question before concluding
  otherwise.
- Being generous with context: what you tried, what you expected, what happened.
- Crediting other people's work, and naming your sources.
- Accepting that a maintainer may decline a change, and asking why rather than
  re-arguing.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks.
- Harassment in public or in private, including repeated contact after a request
  to stop.
- Publishing someone's private information — a physical or email address, a
  workplace, a photograph — without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately wasting reviewers' time: re-opening a settled decision, submitting
  generated noise as a pull request, or filing issues in bad faith.
- Any conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Scope

This code applies in every space this project uses: the repository itself
(issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code comments, review
comments), and any space where someone is representing the project — including
the documentation site, the demo recordings and the LinkedIn posts that carry
the project's name.

It also applies when unacceptable behaviour outside those spaces has a direct
effect on the safety of a participant inside them.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, report it privately to the
maintainer:

- **Primary channel:** a private message to `@krishsharma-code` on GitHub.
- **Fallback:** the email address published on that GitHub profile.

Reports are read by the maintainer only. Please include what happened, where,
when, and whether anyone else was involved; links and screenshots help but are
not required. Reports about the maintainer may be sent to the Datheon program
contact listed on <https://datheon.in>, since a report cannot be adjudicated by
the person it is about.

## What happens to a report

Every report is acknowledged within **5 business days** and handled
confidentially. The maintainer will:

1. Read the report, and ask the reporter for anything missing rather than
   guessing at it.
2. Speak to the people involved, giving the accused a chance to respond.
3. Decide an outcome, and tell the reporter what was decided and why — the
   specifics of a sanction may stay private, but the fact that action was taken
   does not.
4. Record the outcome in the moderation log kept in this repository's private
   maintainer notes, without the reporter's identity.

Retaliation against someone who reports a concern in good faith is itself a
violation of this code, and is treated as the more serious of the two.

## Enforcement guidelines

The maintainer weighs four things: the impact on the people affected, whether
the behaviour was repeated, whether an apology and a change of behaviour
followed, and the power difference between the people involved. The outcomes
below are a guide, not a tariff.

### 1. Correction

**Used for:** a single instance of inappropriate language, or behaviour a
reasonable person would call unprofessional and the author recognises as such.

**Outcome:** a private, written note explaining what was wrong and why it
matters. A public apology may be requested. No further action is taken if the
behaviour stops.

### 2. Warning

**Used for:** a violation through a single incident or a series of actions.

**Outcome:** a warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction
with the people involved for a stated period — including unsolicited contact
with those enforcing the code. Breaking these terms leads to a temporary or
permanent ban.

### 3. Temporary ban

**Used for:** a serious violation, or sustained inappropriate behaviour.

**Outcome:** a temporary ban from any interaction with the project — issues,
pull requests, discussions and reviews — for a stated period. No public or
private contact with the people involved is permitted during the ban. Breaking
the terms leads to a permanent ban.

### 4. Permanent ban

**Used for:** a pattern of violations, harassment of an individual, or
aggression toward or disparagement of a group of people.

**Outcome:** a permanent ban from all participation in this project.
